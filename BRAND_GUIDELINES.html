01 — Brand Philosophy

The Glass Ship

Every surface in DotVerse is translucent. The constellation is always alive beneath. This is not decoration — it is philosophy embedded in every line of CSS.

01
The Aesthetic Reference
Patek Philippe Precision

The words "PATEK PHILIPPE" appear in the typography comment in the source code. Not by accident. DotVerse is a luxury precision instrument — like a fine mechanical watch where you can see the movement through the caseback. Functional beauty with no wasted detail.

Every element earns its place
02
The Name Decoded
Dot + Verse = Universe of Signals

Dot = a single market signal, a data point, a price node. Verse = universe of intelligence, the full field of market context. DotVerse connects what the market leaves scattered — individual signals into a coherent picture that even a beginner can act on.

Connecting the dots of intelligence
03
The Founding Principle
Beginners First. Advanced Second. Always.

A beginner must be able to place their first trade using DotVerse as their only guide. Every "BUY" must come with a plain-English explanation. Every number must have a real-world dollar equivalent. Advanced features — Kelly Criterion, ATR multipliers — live behind "show advanced" toggles. The default surface is always safe, always educational. This principle resolves every design ambiguity: when a feature can be terse or verbose, choose verbose with progressive disclosure.

02 — Color System

Warmth Over
Cold Precision

DotVerse deliberately avoids the cold blue-black of Bloomberg terminals. The palette is warm, deep, and legible — the color of a trading floor at 3am, not a sterile data center.

#07080c
--bg
The Void with Warmth
Not #000000. The 0c blue channel gives this black a microscopic warmth. The space between stars, not a painted wall.
#c9a84c
--gold · --amb · --cr · --orange · --crys · --yg · --topaz
The Brand Soul
7 token aliases, one hex. Muted aged bronze-gold — not yellow #FFD700, not bright amber. Desaturation pulls toward bronze: earned trust, not flashy promise.
#ede8d8
--t1
Cream, Not Clinical White
Pure white on dark backgrounds creates clinical harshness. This cream-tinted warm parchment reads as a premium research house print — not a computer screen.
#5de8a0
--grn
Mint Signal Green
Not lime, not toxic green. Mint — the color of a positive ECG, not a traffic light. A beginner sees BUY and feels confidence and calm, not urgency.
#e8706e
--red
Soft Caution Red
Never #FF0000. Desaturated and warm — communicates caution and attention, not panic. A SELL signal asks the trader to pause and think, not fight-or-flight.
#7c6fe0
--purp
Intelligence Accent
Appears in the logo constellation and on the intelligence layer of the UI. When you see purple, it marks something that required deep computation — confidence labels, AI narrative, analytical depth.

05 — The Decision Journey

MARKET → SIGNAL →
UNDERSTAND → SIZE → ACT

This is not a visual decorative element. It is the mental model DotVerse is building in the trader's mind. Every feature maps to one of these five stages.

01
Market
What is the broader context? Fear/greed, news, correlation with other assets.
02
Signal
What is the system telling me? BUY / SELL / HOLD — from 65% confluence gate.
03
Understand
Why? Confidence level, indicators, plain English reason, MTF alignment.
04
Size
How much should I risk? Calculator, lot sizing, plain-English coaching.
05
Act
Execute, monitor, manage. Pine Script, watchlist, alerts, portfolio tracking.
06 — Voice & Tone

The Knowledgeable Mentor,
Not the Algorithm

DotVerse speaks as someone who knows exactly what they're talking about and wants you to understand it too — never as a system that outputs text.

Signal Cards
"BUY signal — price is above both moving averages and RSI shows momentum building. Entry at $61,540. Your stop loss is below the last swing low at $59,200."
Clear Precise Educational
Coaching / Calculator
"Risking 1% means if this trade hits your stop loss, you lose $100 on a $10,000 account. Your trade continues. At 5%, you'd lose $500 — that's five bad trades away from a 25% drawdown."
Warm Patient Dollar examples always
Error States
"Your stop loss is on the wrong side of your entry for a BUY trade. On a BUY, the stop loss must be below your entry price — it protects you if the price drops."
Direct Solution-oriented Cause + fix, always
Warnings
"Risking 5% per trade is considered aggressive. Most professional traders use 1–2%. At 5%, six consecutive losses would cost you 30% of your account."
Honest Not alarmist Numbers, not adjectives
Non-negotiable voice rules
Never use jargon without a definition nearby. R:R must be followed by "Risk-to-Reward."
Always pair a number with a real-world dollar equivalent in the same sentence.
Never say "invalid" — say what is wrong and exactly how to fix it.
Never leave the user without a next action. Every screen ends with a clear step.
07 — Anti-Patterns

What DotVerse
Deliberately Rejects

These are not style preferences. They are conscious rejections of conventions that contradict the DotVerse philosophy.

Cold blue-black backgrounds (#0F172A)
Reads as Bloomberg/TradingView clone. DotVerse is warm and premium. Background must be #07080c.
Pure white text (#ffffff)
Clinical and harsh. The brand color for text is cream #ede8d8. Never pure white in the app.
Emoji as interface icons (⚡ ⚠ 📊)
Renders inconsistently across platforms. Every icon must be stroke-based inline SVG inheriting currentColor.
Fully opaque panels
Breaks the Glass Ship. The constellation must breathe through every surface. Always use the glass formula.
Signals without explanation
"BUY" alone is not a product. Entry + SL + TP + plain English reason is the minimum viable signal card.
Aggressive defaults (5% risk)
Beginners first. Default risk is 1% with a visible "why 1%?" explainer. Aggression is opt-in, never default.
Bright gold (#FFD700 / #F59E0B)
Yellow reads as cheap cheerfulness. DotVerse gold is #c9a84c — muted, bronze-weighted, earned trust.
Jargon without a definition
DotVerse teaches as it signals. Every technical term must be accompanied by a plain-English gloss.
